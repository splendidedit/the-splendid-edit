The Season · Paris

Pharrell takes Louis Vuitton to the surf

By The Splendid Edit 2 August 2026 Fashion
← Back to The Edit

The Louis Vuitton Men’s Spring-Summer 2027 show set in Paris. Courtesy of Louis Vuitton

A wave stood thirty-seven metres wide on a Paris campus, throwing spray across the front row through a June heatwave. Behind it, Pharrell Williams walked out a Louis Vuitton man dressed for the shore.

Williams has run Louis Vuitton menswear since 2023. His shows work as events first. He builds a set large enough to carry as a single image, seats it with ambassadors and musicians, then lets the collection live inside it. Spring-Summer 2027 kept to that method, with the sea as its subject.

The house showed the collection in late June, in the open air at the Cite Internationale Universitaire de Paris. The floor was fine sand. A curved runway ran out from behind the water. The afternoon was hot, and the spray read as relief.

The seating sat low, close to the sand. The crowd read as music and film as much as front-row fashion, the mix Williams tends to gather. In heat like that, a set built from moving water looked like foresight rather than theatre.

The wave

The wave measured thirty-seven metres across. Williams built it as the scenography, a standing swell that broke and fell for the length of the show. It pulled its colours into the clothes, blues and greens off the surface of the water. Jeremy Allen White, Jackson Wang and J-Hope watched from the sand.

The idea of the coast held the whole set together. Water on one side, shore on the other, a line between them that the models crossed and recrossed. It gave the show a horizon to work against.

Dandy-surfer

Williams called his character a dandy-surfer. The tailoring stayed sharp. Next to it came monogrammed wetsuits cut from Louis Vuitton’s technical fabrics, with a surfboard carried as the season’s accessory. Cloth looked weathered by sun and salt, hand-woven in places, embellished with small marine details.

Surf and skateboarding ran through the graphics. Checkerboard motifs, acid colour, stripes set at the level of the horizon. One jacket was pieced from patches, each a souvenir of a break somewhere in the world. Beads and shells were embroidered on like finds carried home from a beach.

The accessories held the commercial weight. Supple bags, canvas sneakers made to step through sand, baseball caps, hoodies worn with long hair loose. Fluid trousers moved with the walk. A trunk closed the run, its surface catching the light of a low sun.

Colour followed the water. Blue and green held the core of it, warmed by the acid brights of a skate deck and the browns of driftwood. The palette stayed wearable under the noise of the graphics. A checkerboard short set is still a short set.

The soundtrack

Williams scored the show himself. He premiered original songs written for the runway, among them Quavo’s HAAVIN, Lil Baby’s Dead Fresh and a track with YoungBoy Never Broke Again. It closed on Bando, a new song by Angelique Kidjo with Williams and Quavo.

Music and clothes arrived together, as they tend to at his Louis Vuitton. The soundtrack was not a backing track laid under the looks. It was released work, made for the moment the models walked, and it carried the show as much as the set did.

The business

The timing helps. LVMH reported its fashion division back to growth this summer, with Louis Vuitton among the names lifting it. A show on this scale is the marketing end of that engine. It sells the bags in the second row, the caps, the sneakers and the trunk, long after the wave has drained away.

Williams understands the brief. He makes the spectacle that travels, then keeps the products plain enough to move. The dandy-surfer works as a mood board and a sales sheet at the same time.

Closed circuit

The water came from Eaux de Paris and returned to the city’s network through a closed circuit once the show ended. The sand went afterward to the beach volleyball courts at the Cite Internationale Universitaire de Paris. Louis Vuitton tied the staging to its Regeneration 2030 plan.

The house also linked the show to Coral Gardeners, a group replanting reefs in French Polynesia. The gesture sat close to the theme. A collection about the sea, staged on borrowed water, pointed back toward the water it borrowed from.

Pharrell’s Louis Vuitton has always worked at this size. A stadium of an idea, then a rack of clothes people will buy. The wave will outlast most of the looks in memory. The dandy-surfer, in his cap and canvas and patchwork jacket, is the part that ships.
