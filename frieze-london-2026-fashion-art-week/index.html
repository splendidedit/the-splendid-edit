At Frieze, London finds its second fashion week

By The Splendid Edit October 2026 London
← Back to The Edit

The tents are back in the Regent's Park. Frieze London and Frieze Masters opened on 14 October, and the galleries are only half of what the week is about. Around them, the fashion houses have booked the pubs, the department stores and the private dining rooms.

Frieze runs through 18 October. Wednesday was invitation only. The public came from Thursday to Sunday night. More than 170 galleries from more than 40 countries fill the two fairs, contemporary work under one roof, older material across the park at Masters.

The fair is the anchor. The week built around it is the business. London's luxury brands treat these five days as a calendar of their own, set apart from the runway and tuned to a different crowd.

The pattern has hardened over the past few years. The art fair and the fashion week have been edging toward each other for a while, and Frieze week is where the two now meet most plainly. The collector and the luxury client turned out to be the same person.

The fair

Frieze courts the brands openly and lists them as partners. Stone Island returns for its ninth Frieze uniform T-shirt, this one carrying an artwork by Arébénor Bassène and sold through the Corner Shop at Selfridges. The shirt dresses the fair's own staff first, then goes on sale as an object people queue for.

Ruinart continues its Conversations with Nature series with Tadashi Kawamata, its Carte Blanche artist for 2026, and brings its Art Bar to Frieze Masters for the first time. De Beers returns with a second chapter of Voyage Through the Diamond Realm, a display of rough stones that glow under ultraviolet light. illycaffè launches In Minor Keys, a set of cups by four artists who showed at this year's Venice Biennale. Each of them pays for a place inside the tents rather than around them.

The dinners

The louder activity happens past the gates. Vogue mapped a full week of fashion events timed to the fair, and the spread is wide. Church's is taking over a pub. JW Anderson and Ugg are throwing a party. Jimmy Choo and Chanel are both reported to be hosting private dinners.

Acne Studios used 14 October to show a collaboration with Hussein Chalayan, kept private rather than put on a stand. Dover Street Market opened in an Open House format, pulling several designers and projects under one roof for the run of the fair. None of this belongs to Frieze's official gallery program, and the organisers make no such claim. It is the city rearranging itself around the audience the fair brings in.

The logic

The draw is the guest list. Frieze gathers collectors, patrons, advisers and museum people into one corner of London for a week, an audience with taste and money held in the same place. A private dinner reaches fewer people than a runway livestream. It reaches the right ones, for longer, seated.

Location does part of the work. Grosvenor, which owns much of Mayfair, has told Vogue that businesses on Mount Street once saw a double-digit sales lift during Frieze week. That was a single year, and it promises nothing about this one. It does explain why the streets near the park program so heavily when the fair is in town.

The brands have learned to match the access to the aim. Public formats, a pub, a store, an Open House, build discovery and foot traffic. Private formats, the dinner and the preview, build relationships that never show up in a view count. The week lets a house do both at once, a few streets apart.

Each format is measured on its own terms. A store takeover is read in footfall and appointments. An artist collaboration keeps working afterward, in editorial, in film, on a shop floor. A dinner is counted in the names who came, and in what those names do in the months that follow.

After

London Fashion Week is still the city's runway. Frieze has become the other season, smaller and scattered across galleries, hotels and private rooms, sitting closer to collecting than to catwalks. It lands after a full month of spring/summer 2027 shows across the four cities, when the industry has run out of collections to react to and wants a different sort of room.

The fair closes on Sunday. The shirts will have sold, the dinners will have happened, the collaborations will have been photographed and posted. What stays is harder to see on the night. It is whether any of it reads, a season from now, as more than a logo set near a painting.
