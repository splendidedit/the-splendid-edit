Aman’s first Maldives arrives one island at a time

By The Splendid Edit October 2026 5 min read
← Back to The Edit

Amanolu has a name, an atoll and a year. Sixteen of its residences will each sit on an island of their own.

Vaavu Atoll lies roughly 65 kilometres south of Malé, a twenty-minute seaplane hop or an hour and a half by speedboat. Five of its islands are inhabited, which makes it one of the emptiest administrative atolls in the country. Aman has chosen it for its first property in the Maldives, and given it a name: Amanolu, opening in 2028.

The arithmetic is where it gets interesting. The resort runs to 52 keys. Set around it are sixteen Aman Residences, and each one occupies a private island inside the property. Five bedrooms, a 25-metre pool, staff accommodation and a berth of its own.

The plan

Kerry Hill Architects drew it. The studio has been working with Aman for decades, and the house style is familiar from Aman Tokyo and Amanemu: long horizontals, deep eaves, the building kept low enough that the landscape stays in charge. Here the landscape is water, and the drawings let it run between the buildings rather than around them.

The residence islands are not interchangeable. Some face a single shoreline and are oriented to either sunrise or sunset. Others run coast to coast, with a beach on each side and light from both ends of the day. Aman has not said how the sixteen divide between the two.

A double beach island residence at Amanolu, Vaavu Atoll · Courtesy of Aman

Two spare islands

One island in the plan carries no bedrooms at all. It is given over to wellness, with an Aman Spa and the group’s Spa Houses, the freestanding treatment buildings that elsewhere in the portfolio come with their own pools and private terraces. Guests and residence owners both get it.

A second island is given over to the water: diving, snorkelling, watersports, marine exploration. Vaavu earns that. The channels that cut between its reefs and the open ocean pull serious current, and divers have been coming for them since long before anyone was selling real estate here.

The name

Aman is Sanskrit for peace, and the group has now built more than thirty names on it. Olu comes from the water lily, a flower that turns up across the Indian Ocean and carries the usual associations of stillness and renewal. The pairing is tidier than most of the recent ones.

Aman puts its current portfolio at 36 hotels, resorts and branded residences across 21 destinations. Amanolu joins a pipeline that also carries Amansamar in Saudi Arabia, Aman Dubai, Aman Singapore, Aman Miami Beach and Aman Beverly Hills, the last two listed for 2028 as well. Four of those sites did not exist on the group’s map three years ago.

Residence enquiries are open. Room rates are not published, the resort has no month attached to its 2028 opening, and everything released so far is a rendering rather than a photograph. What the drawings do get right is the hour: the light in them is the short one the Maldives gives up just before dusk.
