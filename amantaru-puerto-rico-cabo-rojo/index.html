The Stay · Puerto Rico

Aman finds a hillside in Cabo Rojo

By Isabelle Rowe 20 November 2026 4 min read
← Back to The Edit

Cabo Rojo is the far southwest corner of Puerto Rico, dry and bright, with salt flats and a lighthouse on the cliffs. Aman has chosen it for its fourth address in the Caribbean.

Most visitors to Puerto Rico stay on the San Juan side. The southwest is a two-and-a-half-hour drive away, and it feels like another island: cactus on the headlands, shorebirds on the salt ponds, the lighthouse at Los Morrillos on its limestone bluff. Weekenders from the capital come for the beaches at Boquerón and Playa Sucia. Few others make the trip.

The site

Amantaru will sit on a hillside above a secluded stretch of beach. The land is part of Esencia, a community of more than 2,000 acres that borders a protected nature reserve and wildlife refuge. Aman announced the name this month. In Sanskrit it means peaceful tree.

The architect is Marwan Al-Sayed of MASASTUDIO, and this is his second Aman. The first was Amangiri in the Utah desert, which is still the resort most people think of when they hear the name. For Cabo Rojo he has looked at the island's old Spanish forts and at the coast itself. The renderings show long low pavilions, thick pale walls and deep overhangs against the sun.

The residences

Alongside the resort there will be a small collection of Aman Residences, from three to eight bedrooms. They range from just under 9,000 square feet to nearly 25,000, each with a private pool and wraparound terraces facing the sea. Aman has not said how many there will be.

Each residence has its own pool and terraces facing the sea. Rendering courtesy of Aman

The resort will have an Aman Spa and wellness centre, a beach club, racquet courts and several restaurants, some signature and some local. Owners also get the Aman Private Office and priority for the Aman Club, the members' houses in New York, Tokyo and Bangkok.

The rest of Esencia

Three Rules Capital and the Reuben Brothers are behind Esencia. The plan includes two championship golf courses, one of them El Capitán by Rees Jones, plus an equestrian centre, field and racquet clubs, trails and a bilingual school. Mandarin Oriental and Rosewood are each building their own resorts on the land.

A private airfield with a 6,700-foot runway is about five minutes away, and the master plan includes a dark-sky lighting scheme. No opening date or room count has been given yet. The other Aman resorts in the region are Amanyara and Amanera, with Amancaya in the Bahamas still to come.
