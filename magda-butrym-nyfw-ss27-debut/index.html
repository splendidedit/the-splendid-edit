The Season / New York

Magda Butrym brings Warsaw to Mercer Street.

By The Splendid Edit September 2026 Fashion
← Back to The Edit

A townhouse on Mercer Street opened this month with rose motifs worked into the walls and thread the colour of dried petals. On the thirteenth, the label behind it walks the official New York schedule for the first time. Magda Butrym spent twelve years building toward both.

The boutique sits at 170 Mercer, three floors inside a cast-iron stretch of SoHo. Stamuli, a studio based in Stockholm, drew the rooms. The brief treated the space as a set of rooms to move through, arranged around craft and atmosphere rather than a single sales floor. A shopper enters something closer to a private apartment than a flagship.

Mercer Street

The interiors hold two places in the same frame. SoHo's industrial bones stay exposed, the columns and the light of a nineteenth-century block. Against them the studio set vintage furniture, quiet corners and the rose that recurs through every Butrym collection. Polish craftsmanship is written into the materials, and the label describes the result as a world to discover rather than a store to cross.

Mercer Street is a deliberate address. The block carries the fashion trade of the neighbourhood, and it sits a short walk from the flagships that set SoHo's tone. A three-floor footprint on that stretch reads as a statement of intent, sized for a house that until now leaned on other people's shelves.

The store opened in early September, days ahead of the runway. That order was chosen. It lets a visitor handle the clothes on Mercer before the collection reaches the schedule, and it gives the week a single shape instead of two separate announcements.

Warsaw

Butrym started the house in the Polish capital roughly twelve years ago. The signatures came early. Sculptural shoulders, dresses cut close and finished by hand, flowers built in three dimensions and stitched to the body. The work is romantic without turning weightless, and it has sold through the luxury multibrand floors and the major e-stores for years.

The flowers are the tell. Butrym moulds them in fabric, layers the petals and fixes them to a shoulder or a hip so they sit like sculpture rather than print. Boning and close seaming hold the silhouettes upright. It is couture logic applied to clothes made to be sold and worn, and it explains why the price sits where it does.

The New York address is one stop in a longer expansion. A Warsaw boutique opened recently, part of a run that has carried a designer known to buyers toward shoppers who buy for themselves. The American market has always mattered to the house, and until now it reached that market mostly through wholesale. Mercer Street changes the terms of the relationship.

Butrym has described New York as a city of contrasts, direct and emotional at once, and has framed the boutique as a personal marker more than a commercial one. She has spoken of wanting a place where a woman can meet the brand up close. The language matches the space. Intimacy is the argument, in a market that tends to reward scale.

The schedule

On 13 September the label shows on the official New York calendar for the first time. Butrym has presented in Paris before, at the edges of the week and then inside it. Choosing New York for this debut is a decision about audience. It sets the collection in front of the American editors and buyers who already stock the label, in the city where the store now stands.

The placement sits inside a wider season of arrivals. New York's Spring/Summer 2027 schedule runs thick with returns and first appearances, from Diane von Furstenberg's reopening under Henry Zankov to Sabyasachi's homecoming after twenty years. Butrym joins that current from the outside, a European house planting itself on the calendar rather than a native name coming back.

The logic is commercial as much as creative. A label built on wholesale gains little from a Paris slot that competes with the largest houses in the world. In New York it can hold a morning of its own, court the buyers who already carry the line, and tie the week to a store those same buyers can walk into.

The bet

Soft, romantic dressing is a crowded register this season, and it fills the boards across the week. Butrym's case for it is the hand behind the clothes. The draping, the built flowers, the finish that only reads at close range. A backstage table of thread, pins and half-pinned muslin says more about the house than any lookbook, and the store exists to put that within reach.

Twelve years in, the house lands in New York with a boutique, a runway slot and a settled idea of the woman it dresses. The collection on the thirteenth will test the timing. The rooms on Mercer are already taking visitors. For once the sequence runs backward, and the city can shop the label before it sees the show.
