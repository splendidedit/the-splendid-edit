Burberry puts the trench behind glass

By The Splendid Edit 19 September 2026 Fashion
← Back to The Edit

London Fashion Week ends on a Monday this September, and it ends inside a museum. On 21 September, the last day of the schedule, Burberry opens a free display at the V&A. The coat that made the house steps off the runway and into the Prince Consort Gallery.

The display is called The Burberry Trench: Crafting an Icon. It runs at V&A South Kensington from 21 September to 3 January 2027. Admission costs nothing. That last detail is the point. A fashion week built on invitation lists closes with a room anyone can walk into.

Burberry timed it to the house's 170th anniversary. Thomas Burberry founded the company in 1856. He invented gabardine in 1879, a tightly woven cloth that kept the weather out without the weight of rubberised fabric. The trench grew from military dress and functional outerwear into something people now wear without thinking about the rain at all.

The coat as system

What the V&A shows is less a single garment than a set of parts. The collar. The belt. The storm flap across the shoulder. The epaulettes. The D-rings. Each element began as a solution to a practical problem, and each survived long after the problem faded. You can stand close to them here and see the seams.

The display moves through the coat's creative eras. Christopher Bailey stretched it into the check-lined, festival-ready Burberry of the 2010s. Riccardo Tisci cut it sharper and darker. Daniel Lee has pulled it back toward the equestrian and the English countryside. Three designers, one silhouette, no loss of recognition.

That is the argument the room makes without stating it. The trench holds its identity while everything around it changes. A creative director can alter the fabric, the proportion, the styling, and the coat still reads as itself. Few products in fashion carry that kind of structural memory.

Heritage in public

The timing matters. A runway show lasts twelve minutes and then lives on in photographs. A museum display holds the conversation for three and a half months. Burberry gets a story that outlasts the front row, and London gets proof that its fashion week reaches past the tents.

London has always drawn its power from more than clothes. Music, art schools, nightlife, museums and street culture all feed the same current. A trench display at the V&A sits inside that ecosystem cleanly. It presents fashion as design history while staying tied to a working commercial house.

For visitors the practical details are simple. The Prince Consort Gallery, from 21 September through 3 January, free of charge, on the closing day of a schedule that ran from 17 September. Check the V&A listing before travelling in case the access notes shift.

London Fashion Week closes on 21 September, when Burberry opens its trench display at the V&A. Courtesy of Fashion PR Firm

The most durable objects in fashion tend to be the ones people already own. The trench is one of them. Putting it behind glass is a way of saying so out loud, in a room where a student and a buyer can stand at the same seam and read it the same way.
