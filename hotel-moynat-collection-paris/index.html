At Moynat, the hotel labels come off the trunk

By The Splendid Edit October 2026 Paris
← Back to The Edit

A hundred years ago, a trunk kept a record of where it had been. Porters and front desks stuck paper labels to the lid, one hotel at a time, until the case read like a passport. Moynat has taken those stickers off the old trunks and sewn them onto a new collection.

The project is called Hotel Moynat, and its first chapter reached the house's boutiques this month. Moynat built it with the stylist Storm Pablo, known for his work with Bad Bunny, and the American jewellery designer Marvin Douglas. The brief was simple on paper. Take the archive of hotel labels and bring them forward without turning the result into a museum case.

The labels

Vintage hotel labels were small works of graphic design. Each one named a property and a city, often with a drawing of the facade or a flag or a palm, printed in a few bright colours. They were made to be stuck on and peeled off, disposable by nature. Moynat has done the opposite and made them permanent.

For this first chapter the labels are recreated in leather and stitched one by one onto the house's signature "M" Monogram canvas. The colours and the graphics carry across a run of bags and accessories. A case you once labelled by travelling now arrives already covered, the itinerary built into the surface before it leaves the shop.

That inversion is the whole idea. The old trunk earned its stickers over years of wear. The new bag wears them from the start, which turns a private travel diary into a pattern anyone can buy. It is a knowing move, and the collection does not pretend otherwise.

The collaborators

Storm Pablo and Marvin Douglas were not hired to redraw the archive line for line. They picked elements of vintage luggage and read them through their own eyes, which pulls the house toward music, styling and contemporary culture rather than heritage alone. Moynat has leaned on outside voices before, and the logic here is the same. A trunk-maker founded in 1849 keeps its relevance by letting newer hands touch the codes.

The house sits among LVMH's leather-goods maisons and trades on a quieter history than most of them. Moynat predates the better-known Paris luggage names and built its reputation on lightweight, curved trunks before the monogram era. Handing the labels to a stylist who dresses a reggaeton superstar is a way of saying the archive can still travel in company it was never designed for.

The range

The collection spreads the labels across a wide set of shapes. Hobo bags and totes carry the pattern at scale, while clutches, passport holders and charms take it in smaller doses. There are silk scarves, a new Mignon bag, and a set of exclusive Limousine trunks that push the idea back onto the travel pieces where it started.

That spread matters. A motif this specific can wear thin if it only lives on one hero bag. By running it from a charm up to a full trunk, Moynat gives a buyer a way in at several price points and keeps the concept from reading as a single gimmick. The passport holder and the Limousine trunk tell the same story at opposite ends of the shelf.

A second chapter is already set for November. It trades the paper-label reference for the hardware, recreating the metal fittings that once protected the corners of a trunk as crystal studs applied to the same "M" Monogram canvas. The house is working through the anatomy of old luggage one part at a time, labels first, then the fittings.

The first chapter is in Moynat boutiques now, with the second arriving at selected points of sale in the coming weeks. Read together, they make a case for a trunk-maker that would rather mine its own past than borrow someone else's. The labels were always the most human thing about a piece of luggage. Moynat has made them the point.
