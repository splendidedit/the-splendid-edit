The Season / Paris

Carven trusts a studio hand

By The Splendid Edit 27 August 2026 Fashion
← Back to The Edit

The name most people did not know now runs one of the oldest small houses in Paris. Carven has given its studio to Kai Nesselrath, and the first proof of the choice arrives in October, on a runway rather than in a lookbook.

Nesselrath spent close to a decade at Saint Laurent. He rose through Anthony Vaccarello's studio to head of womenswear, the person who sits behind the collection rather than in front of the camera. Before that came a short passage at Chanel. Before that, an unusual training: Polimoda in Florence, the Accademia di Belle Arti in Rome, courses at Central Saint Martins in London. Italian by birth, German by descent, schooled across three cities.

Carven confirmed him as design director this week. WWD had reported the move back in April, when Mark Thomas left after a single year in the seat. The house let the vacancy sit through the summer and named its replacement with the September shows already on the calendar.

A small house

Carven was never one of the giants, and that was the point of it. Marie-Louise Carven, born Marie-Louise Carmen de Tommaso, opened the house in 1945 in an atelier at the Rond-Point des Champs-Elysees. She stood five foot one. She built her line for women her own size, on the argument that the couturiers of the day cut everything for tall mannequins and left the rest to guess. Green and white became her signature. Ma Griffe, the perfume, arrived a year later and carried the name further than the clothes ever did.

The house passed through several hands after her. Guillaume Henry turned it into a contemporary favourite in the early 2010s, all schoolgirl proportion and clean colour, before that chapter closed. Bankruptcy came in 2018. The Chinese group Icicle bought the label that year, paying just over four million euros with a promise of far more in investment, and began the slow work of putting it back together.

The current revival started in 2023. The stated ambition is to return to the 1945 idea: French, precise, made for a real body rather than a runway abstraction. That is a specific brief, and it explains the hire. A studio designer trained on proportion and cut is a closer fit for that house than a stylist chasing a spectacle.

A house built for one particular body has to decide whose eye to trust with it. Carven chose the person who was already doing the work.

October

The Federation de la Haute Couture et de la Mode lists the Spring/Summer 2027 womenswear season from Monday 28 September to Tuesday 6 October. Nesselrath is set to show early in that run, on 1 October, the same day the calendar carries Chloe, Balenciaga, Rick Owens, Schiaparelli and Isabel Marant. A first collection on a crowded Thursday is a hard place to be seen. It is also the fastest way to find out whether the studio hand can hold a room.

Shawna Tao, the Carven chief executive, framed the appointment around a new generation and a lighter mood for the house. Nesselrath has spoken of coming to the role with optimism. The words are the easy part. The green and white, the short straight line, the case that a small house still has a reason to exist in a Paris of megabrands, all of that has to read on the clothes in five weeks.

Carven has been rebuilt before and lost again. What it has not had in years is a designer whose whole training points at the exact thing the house says it wants. That is the wager on the table. On 1 October it stops being a wager and becomes a collection.
