Armani gives Emporio to an outsider, and trades the runway for a room

By The Splendid Edit September 2026 Milan
← Back to The Edit

Armani has handed Emporio to someone who does not share its name. Dario Vitale arrives from Versace, and before that Miu Miu, with a brief that spans the young line and the accessories that carry the main label. His first Milan appointment under the house is a presentation, not a show.

The announcement came on 16 September. Armani named Vitale creative director of Emporio Armani and Giorgio Armani Accessories, effective at once. He is the first designer from outside the family to take a creative role of this weight. Reuters carried the news, the Associated Press framed it as the opening move in a wider reorganization. It landed six days before Milan Fashion Week opened.

Vitale knows the city already. He spent close to fifteen years at Miu Miu, ending as design director of ready-to-wear and head of image. He then took the top job at Versace in the spring of 2025 and showed one collection, Spring/Summer 2026, that September. A single season there, and then this. The step reads as a deliberate one, made from a position of choice rather than repair.

The room

Emporio Armani presents on Thursday, from nine in the morning until five in the afternoon, at Via Bergognone 59. That all-day format replaces the runway the line usually stages for its summer collection. A show compresses a season into minutes. One entrance, one soundtrack, one finale, and the verdict is half-written before the lights come up.

A day-long presentation moves at another pace. Editors and buyers can walk the pieces, turn a bag over in the hand, read a seam up close. The choice suits a house mid-transition. It lets Armani introduce Vitale's remit without staging it as a coronation. Some of the collection predates him, and the company has not said how much.

Calling Thursday his full debut would run ahead of what anyone can confirm. The honest reading is narrower. A new designer joins two categories, and the first public sight of his hand may be partial. That is worth saying plainly, at a moment when every gesture invites a headline.

The family

Armani has kept its core in place. Silvana Armani continues to guide the principal womenswear. Leo Dell'Orco holds the menswear. Vitale sits alongside them with Emporio and accessories, not above them. The structure reads as addition, not replacement.

An outside designer takes two important categories while the people who worked closest to Giorgio Armani keep the main collections. That balance matters after the year the house has had. Giorgio Armani died in September 2025, aged ninety-one. He had run the company as founder, designer and owner for five decades, a level of control almost unheard of at that scale.

The line

Emporio has always sat between the two registers Armani built. Below the main label, above the diffusion pieces, aimed at a younger buyer who wants the eagle without the couture price. It moves quickly, sells widely, and carries the accessories that many people meet the house through first. Handing it to Vitale, alongside the bags and shoes of the Giorgio Armani label, puts a single point of view across the parts of the business that reach furthest.

His record fits that job. At Miu Miu he shaped image as much as product, in a period when the label turned into one of the most talked-about names in Milan. That instinct for how a young line reads, in a shop window and on a phone screen, is the thing Armani appears to be buying.

The stake

His will asked his heirs to sell a fifteen percent share of the company within eighteen months of his death. Ownership and design are separate questions. They now sit inside the same story. Every appointment and every format change gets read as a signal about where the house is heading.

Precision helps here. Vitale's arrival is confirmed. The shift in Emporio's format is confirmed. Anything past that, that the presentation previews a sale, that it marks a commercial pivot, that it settles the group's long-term shape, is guesswork. The credible version stays modest. Armani has brought in an experienced designer for two categories and left its principal lines with the people who know them.

Thursday

A few things will tell the real story. The credits, and how plainly the house describes Vitale's part in the work. The accessories, and whether bags and shoes take a larger role now that one designer holds them across both labels. The codes, and which Emporio signatures serve as anchors through the change. The wording of the official notes, whether they speak of evolution or of a break.

Those signals carry more than a job title does. A change of direction shows itself in staging, in access, in what a house chooses to put first. Milan will read all of it closely. The nine-to-five window gives more people the chance to look, and more time to decide what they make of it.

The week runs through 28 September, and Emporio is one appointment among sixty. It may prove the one that lingers. Armani spent fifty years making every decision himself. Thursday is the first real look at how the house makes them without him.
