Louis Vuitton settles on a new signature

By The Splendid Edit 8 August 2026 Fashion
← Back to The Edit

A bag rarely earns a name on its first outing. Louis Vuitton has given the Multipass one anyway, and set it beside the Petite Malle and the Side Trunk as a fixture rather than a season.

The Multipass arrived with the Cruise 2026 collection, shown in Avignon. Nicolas Ghesquiere designed a soft, slouching shape, closer to an everyday carryall than to the hard-sided trunks the house built its name on. A year on, it has moved from a runway curiosity to one of the pieces the Maison now leans on.

Louis Vuitton has a small canon of bags it treats as permanent. The Petite Malle took the trunk and shrank it to the size of a clutch. The Side Trunk turned a corner of the archive into a daily object. Adding a third to that list is a decision, not an accident. The house does it seldom, and only when it believes a shape can outlast the collection it came from.

Cruise collections travel by design. Louis Vuitton stages them mid-year in a chosen city, away from the Paris calendar, and uses the setting to try out ideas it wants to carry into the seasons that follow. Avignon gave the Multipass its debut. The choice of a Cruise show, rather than a September runway, was its own quiet signal that the bag was built for the long run.

The shape

The body is unstructured. It folds and gives where the trunks hold their line. That softness is the point. It reads as lived-in from the first day, the kind of bag that looks better with a crease than without one.

Nothing about it announces the house from across a room, unless it is cut in Monogram. The form does the talking. After a decade of hard cases and rigid corners across the accessories wall, a bag that slumps is a shift in posture for Ghesquiere, who has led the women's studio since 2013.

How it carries

Three straps do the work. A removable shoulder strap, a braided chain, and a short top handle let the bag sit on the shoulder, across the body, in the hand, or looped on the wrist. One bag, four ways to wear it. That convertible logic is what Louis Vuitton is betting will keep it in rotation past the first month.

It is a practical argument dressed as a fashion one. A piece that adapts to the day gets carried more, and a bag that gets carried more is a bag people talk themselves into buying. The Maison has read that behaviour correctly before.

The materials

The first run covers the house codes and then reaches past them. Monogram canvas for the people who want it read at a glance. Grained leather in a spread of tones, from deep black to a soft vanilla. A suede treatment for something quieter.

The most interesting version is the least obvious. Louis Vuitton prints an Epi trompe-l'oeil onto suede calfskin, a flat surface made to look like the raised Epi grain the house has used since the 1980s. It is a small trick, and a telling one. The Maison is playing with its own archive rather than repeating it.

The mini

For Autumn/Winter 2026 the house shrank it. The Mini Multipass keeps the shape and the straps in a smaller frame. In Monogram it starts around 2,230 dollars, which sets the floor for the family and puts the name within reach of a first Louis Vuitton bag.

Price matters here more than it looks. A signature only works if it can be met at more than one point. The full size holds the top of the range; the Mini opens a door lower down, where a customer buys in and, the house hopes, comes back.

The signal

The timing is not incidental. Louis Vuitton sits inside a group whose fashion and leather goods division returned to organic growth in the second quarter of 2026, after a soft stretch for luxury. Leather goods carried much of that recovery. A bag designed to be bought and worn every day is exactly the kind of product a house reaches for when it wants dependable demand rather than a viral moment.

The convertible bag is a crowded idea at the moment. Rival houses have spent the past two years selling straps that swap and shapes that shift from tote to clutch. Louis Vuitton comes late to that particular conversation and arrives with scale on its side, a distribution network and a set of house materials few can match. Being second to an idea has rarely cost the Maison much when it owns the finish.

Names get handed out easily in fashion. Signatures are earned in reorders, in the second and third purchase, in the bag a customer reaches for without thinking. The Multipass has the name. The next year will show whether it has the rest.
