The Season / Paris

Four hundred and forty shows, one shopping list

By The Splendid Edit 19 October 2026 Business
← Back to The Edit

Fashion month produced four hundred and forty shows. The clothes have been seen. Now the buyers decide which of them stores will actually carry.

Vogue Business published its global buyer survey on 14 October. Retail executives from New York, London, Milan and Paris named the pieces they expect to turn runway attention into orders. The answers were specific. Skirts. Expressive shoes. Colour. Lace and broderie anglaise. Waists pulled in and defined.

These are forecasts, not sales figures. Their worth lies in what stores are preparing to support before the customer has spoken.

The skirt

The clearest signal is the skirt. Buyers have started treating it as a category to merchandise rather than a styling trick. Prada's midi lengths came up again and again. So did the printed versions at Dries Van Noten. A skirt lets a customer refresh what she already owns without committing to a whole runway look, and that makes it easy to put on a shop floor.

The shoe

Footwear carried almost as much weight. Chanel showed eighty-three shoe styles. Buyers flagged Celine's jazz-inspired shoe and a run of stranger shapes elsewhere as likely winners. In bags, Alaïa's Frame clutch landed among the projected bestsellers. The common thread is recognisability. Each piece reads at once, on a shop floor or in a campaign frame.

Around those sit the rest of the signals. The report records appetite for maximalism, strong colour, lace, broderie anglaise and a cinched silhouette. The season is not a retreat into plainness. Buyers want pieces that carry feeling and can still be explained in a sentence, then worn more than once.

The verdict

Paris finished as the buyers' favourite city, with New York close behind. Paris held the largest houses and the most watched creative directions. New York answered with directness and a clean commercial logic. The distance between the two was narrow this time.

At house level, Chanel and Celine drew the strongest response across ready-to-wear and accessories. Saint Laurent's gilded finale, Anthony Vaccarello's last before he left the house, was also expected to sell. These are projections. They point to where retailers believe brand authority and clear product are lining up.
