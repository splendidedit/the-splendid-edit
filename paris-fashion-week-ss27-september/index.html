Cover Story / Paris

Paris keeps the last word

By The Splendid Edit 23 August 2026 Fashion
← Back to The Edit

The calendar reaches Paris last and stays the longest. Nine days, sixty-eight shows, more than a hundred houses on the schedule. When Louis Vuitton takes the last slot on 6 October, the season has its shape.

The Fédération de la Haute Couture et de la Mode has set the provisional calendar for Spring/Summer 2027. Womenswear runs Monday 28 September through Tuesday 6 October, with sixty-eight runway shows and a run of presentations across the nine days. Maxhosa Africa opens the week on the 28th, its first show on the official Paris calendar, the label founded by the South African designer Laduma Ngxokolo.

Paris arrives after New York, London and Milan have each had their turn. By the time the city opens, editors and buyers have already sat through three weeks of collections. Paris asks them to keep going for nine more days, and it holds the names that carry the most weight. Dior, Chanel, Saint Laurent, Balenciaga, Loewe, Miu Miu, Louis Vuitton, Celine, Tom Ford, Dries Van Noten.

Half of that list now reads as work in progress. Jonathan Anderson shows his Dior on 29 September. Matthieu Blazy is early into his Chanel. Pierpaolo Piccioli is building Balenciaga. Jack McCollough and Lazaro Hernandez are finding the shape of Loewe. Julian Klausner carries Dries Van Noten forward, and Michael Rider keeps drawing Celine in daylight. A season this crowded with new signatures does not come around often.

The debuts

Two houses hand over the keys this week. Kai Nesselrath shows a first Carven on 1 October. Drew Henry steps out for Courrèges on 30 September. Both walk in with a house history behind them and a single collection to make the case. The room decides the rest in about twelve minutes.

Maison Margiela comes back to the Paris calendar after showing in Shanghai last season, Glenn Martens returning the house to its home schedule. Valentino is back on the list too. The provisional calendar always shifts before the week opens, so slots move and names come and go, but the frame is set.

The crush

Some days carry more than a schedule should. The 30th of September lines up The Row, Balmain, Dries Van Noten, Stella McCartney, Acne Studios and Tom Ford, and asks editors to be in six places between morning and night. This is the part of the week nobody photographs. Cars, side streets, a seat held two shows ahead.

Sunday brings its own order. Miu Miu takes a new Sunday slot on 4 October, a house that has spent the last few seasons setting the terms of the conversation. The night then closes with Valentino, Mugler and Jean Paul Gaultier in sequence. By Monday the week has one show left to give.

What Paris settles

The city works as the season’s verdict. What shows in Paris tends to set the shape of next spring, the line that other houses answer, the bag that turns up on every account. A strong Paris week can steady a house that stumbled in Milan. A weak one travels just as far.
