Fashion Commentary / Paris

At Alaïa, the atelier takes the season

By The Splendid Edit October 2026 Paris
← Back to The Edit

Alaïa showed Spring/Summer 2027 in Paris on the third of October. There was no name on the invitation, no designer to bow at the end. The studio made the collection, and the studio stood behind it.

The house staged two intimate evening presentations rather than a runway. The Fédération de la Haute Couture et de la Mode listed Alaïa as a presentation on that date, in the closing stretch of the season. The format was quiet by design. It asked the room to look at the clothes and the hands that made them, not at a new face in the front row.

The exit

Pieter Mulier led Alaïa from 2021. He took a house built on the memory of its founder and turned it into a visible, growing business. His silhouette was sculptural and his branding stayed light, a rare combination in a market that sells on logos.

Richemont confirmed in January that he would leave after his final Paris show. That show came in March, a swansong read as a close rather than a stumble. His tenure left a set of objects the street recognised: the rounded Barrel jeans, the mesh ballet flats, the long Teckel bag. Each one was copied within a season of its release.

Le Monde reported that the studio would steer the house until a successor arrived. The wording was deliberate. It framed Spring/Summer 2027 as planned continuity, a season held in place on purpose, not a gap filled at the last minute.

The studio

A collection is never one person's work. Pattern cutters, drapers, knit specialists, leather artisans and product developers turn an idea into something a body can wear. A creative director gives the house a public face and a final edit. The studio holds the deeper knowledge of fit, technique and what the ateliers can actually build.

At Alaïa that knowledge is the asset. The house was founded on an exact relationship with the body, on tension and movement and cut. Those qualities live in the hands of the people who make the clothes. They do not transfer to a campaign or a slogan.

So a season designed by the studio is a fair test of what the name is worth without a star attached. The people who draped the last ten years of Alaïa draped this one too. The continuity is real, held in muscle memory as much as in any archive.

The body

Azzedine Alaïa built his reputation on the figure. He cut close, he worked in jersey and leather and knit, he fitted a garment until it moved with the person inside it. The house he left behind is studied for that discipline, and the Association that keeps his archive treats the cut as the lesson.

That history gives the studio a clear brief. Hold the architecture. Keep the knit exact. Let the bags and the shoes grow without losing the shapes clients already know. These are decisions about craft before they are decisions about taste, and craft is what this house keeps in-house.

The stakes

Continuity is not the same as caution. The studio still had to choose which codes to repeat, which recent hits to protect, and how much newness the market expects from a house mid-transition. A season that only copies the last one reads as nervous. A season that strays too far loses the thread.

The commercial task is narrow. Alaïa needs to reassure its clients and its wholesale partners while the search for a director runs its course. Richemont has not named one in public. Every strong season the studio delivers buys the group time; every weak one shortens it.

The people who read these shows for a living know where to look. They watch whether the cut still carries the house's physical authority. They watch the knitwear, where Alaïa has always made its case, and the bags and shoes, which carry the recent commercial weight. They watch the balance between pieces made to be photographed and pieces made to be sold. The answers to those questions tell the market more than any announcement would.

A house that can show well without a name on the door is a house that understood its own value all along.

The Splendid Edit

Luxury groups like to call their houses institutions with codes that outlast any one designer. They then spend most of their marketing on the designer. Alaïa's studio season puts that claim to the test in public. If the clothes hold, the argument holds with them.

The next chapter still waits on an appointment. Until it comes, the people who cut and drape and knit the house will keep it moving. That is the quiet point of the whole exercise. The body of knowledge was always the house. The season just made it visible.
