The Stay · Seoul

The Shilla keeps a state guest house in the garden

By Elena Voss 13 November 2026 4 min read
← Back to The Edit

The Shilla has stood on the lower slope of Namsan since March 1979. In its garden is the house where Korea once put up visiting presidents.

Take the path behind the hotel before breakfast. It climbs through pines and sculpture by Korean artists towards the old city wall. On the way down, the roofs of Yeong Bin Gwan come into view: curved tile, green and red paint under the eaves. The building is older than the hotel beside it.

The house

Yeong Bin Gwan dates to 1967. The government built it for state receptions, and in its years as the official guest house close to forty heads of state stayed there. Lee Byung-chul, the founder of Samsung, took it over in 1973. Six years later he opened a hotel next door and named it after the ancient kingdom of Shilla, whose gold crowns still turn up on Korean posters.

The guest house is still in use. Two of its floors are built in the traditional Korean manner, and the garden outside seats eight hundred for dinner. Seoul holds its society weddings here. Song Joong-ki and Song Hye-kyo married in the hall in October 2017, in front of two hundred and fifty guests.

The lobby

Little of the 1979 interior survives. The hotel closed for seven months and reopened on 1 August 2013 with interiors by Peter Remedios, who worked in leather, wood and dark marble and removed the smallest rooms altogether. The lobby and lounge stayed as one double-height volume. Thirteen years on, it has aged well.

Above it hangs a cloud of acrylic beads by the sculptor Bahk Seon-ghi. The piece has been there since 2006, and he rehangs it in a new shape most years. The Library works as lobby lounge and bar, with a whisky room called The Distillers Library to one side. A pastry boutique off the lobby sells to the city as well as to guests.

The double-height lobby lounge, seen from above. Courtesy of Splendid Magazine

Floor 23

The twenty-third floor holds the best rooms in the building that are not bedrooms. La Yeon cooks Korean food and holds two Michelin stars. Continental, the French restaurant, has been up here since the hotel opened. Lower down, Palsun does Chinese, Ariake does Japanese and The Parkview runs all day.

The Executive Lounge shares the floor with La Yeon, Seoul spread out below the glass. It feels like a private drawing room. Breakfast starts at 06:30, afternoon tea runs from 15:30 to 17:00, and happy hour goes from 18:00 until 22:00. After nine the kitchen narrows to drinks, cheese and dessert.

The tea trolley draws on the pastry kitchen downstairs, and it shows. The room is small and fills on weekend evenings, so go up at six. Children under thirteen are admitted only on weekend and holiday afternoons, between noon and three.

The rooms

There are 464 rooms and suites. The Executive Grand Deluxe is the one to book: fifty-three square metres on floors seven to twenty-two, with lounge access included. Rooms face either Namsan or the city. Ask for the city side and a high floor.

Downstairs there is an indoor pool, and an outdoor one with cabanas that opens from late March to mid-November. The spa is run with Clé de Peau Beauté, and there is a gym, a sauna and an indoor golf range. Families get a Kids’ Lounge on the fifth floor, free for guests travelling with young children.

The hotel shuttle runs to Dongguk University station, Myeongdong and Dongdaemun. Incheon is about ninety minutes by taxi. The Shilla holds a Michelin Key and has kept its Forbes five stars eight years running. Locals still book it when the occasion counts.
