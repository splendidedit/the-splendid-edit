The Season / New York

Diane von Furstenberg opens New York

By The Splendid Edit 2 September 2026 Fashion
← Back to The Edit

Diane von Furstenberg has not staged a runway show since 2017. On Thursday the house returns, and the collection belongs to Henry Zankov, a knitwear designer who worked inside the company years ago and now directs it. His show opens the official New York calendar.

The placement is deliberate. Zankov's debut is the first beat of Spring/Summer 2027, the moment the season starts. For a brand transition that has been watched since spring, the opening slot turns a change of designer into the story New York wakes up to.

The opener

The Council of Fashion Designers of America published its preliminary schedule on 1 July and put the Diane von Furstenberg show at the front. It runs on 10 September. The official calendar continues through 15 September with around seventy shows and presentations. Thom Browne holds the closing slot.

The first day carries its own weight. Editors and buyers arrive before the week wears them down, before the scheduling conflicts and the content backlog set in. A strong opener sets the early tone for the whole season. It also raises the bar the middle of the week gets measured against.

The calendar is still provisional, and teams are told to confirm timing with the CFDA before call sheets close. The morning after Zankov shows carries an adjustment of its own. That Friday marks twenty-five years since 11 September, and the New York shows begin at 10 a.m. in acknowledgement of the date.

A return

Zankov's appointment was announced on 27 May. He is not arriving cold. He worked at Diane von Furstenberg through the 2010s before leaving to build his own label under his surname. This time the brief is wider. It reaches across the house's creative identity, not the collections alone.

His independent line built a following on colour and knit. He works in bold pattern and natural fibres, and points to art, architecture, sport and street culture as sources. He will keep that label running while he leads DVF. The two roles sit side by side, which makes September an early test of how cleanly he can hold them apart.

The wrap dress

Diane von Furstenberg built the company on a single design. The wrap dress arrived in 1974, cut in jersey, closed with a tie rather than buttons or a zip, and made for a woman getting dressed in a hurry. It sold in the millions and fixed her name in American fashion. Every chapter since has been read against it.

That history is an asset and a burden at once. The wrap dress will shape part of the conversation on 10 September whether the collection leans on it or steps away. The harder work sits in the wardrobe around it. Zankov has to carry the founder's principle, clothes built for a life in motion, into pieces she never drew.

Diane von Furstenberg's own standing in New York adds to the interest. She led the CFDA for more than a decade and remains one of the industry's recognised figures in the city. A house tied that closely to the American calendar returning to it as the opener makes a neat piece of symmetry.

What to watch

The nine-year absence gives the debut unusual charge. This is the first DVF runway show since 2017 and the first full look at a new creative structure for a familiar American label. Zankov's colour sense is proven. Whether the collection reads as Diane von Furstenberg, rather than as his own label under a borrowed name, is the question the room will bring.

Buyers will look past the images to the product. Delivery dates, categories, price and the store plan decide whether an opening-day success turns into a season. Heritage fills the seats. A reason to buy now is what keeps the customer.

The facts are settled. Zankov has the job, his debut opens New York on 10 September, and the house is back on a runway after a long silence. Everything else waits on the clothes. The season starts not with a verdict but with a designer walking into a name most of the industry can already picture.
