The Stay · Halkidiki

Fifty summers above the Aegean

By Isabelle Rowe 25 November 2026 4 min read
← Back to The Edit

In 1975 the cheapest land in Halkidiki was the land beside the sea. Danai Riefenstahl bought a slope of it, opened four rooms the next summer, and this year her hotel ran its fiftieth season.

The Danai sits on a pine-covered cliff on Sithonia, the middle of Halkidiki’s three fingers, between Nikiti and Metamorfosi. Land on this coast was once priced by its olive trees, and the salt soil grew thin ones. Danai and her husband Otto, a Berliner, opened House Danai in 1976 with four rooms and beds built from brick. Supplies came up the hill by horse and cart.

The family still runs it. The hotel has grown the way a village does, in white marble residences that step down through pines two centuries old, with a chapel, a library, a tennis court and a lane of shops the regulars call Danai 5th Avenue. There are fifty-six rooms now and no two match.

The anniversary

The family marked fifty years with builders. Since 2023 it has redone its two best restaurants, cut a wine house into the hill and opened a spa. For the anniversary it hired Muza Lab, the London studio of Inge Moore and Nathan Hutchins, to rework the pool, the beach and a new set of suites. The studio said it set out to keep the soul of the house and give it a modern reading.

The Pool Suites are the rooms to book. They sit on the ground floor, 60 square metres with one bedroom or 108 with two. Each has a private garden, a heated pool of its own and a wall of glass that slides back onto the vines. Inside it is greens, blues and teal against pale timber, with carved wood and mother-of-pearl made by local craftsmen.

The new main pool is tiled in a loud pattern. Below it, 300 metres of private sand are lined with rope-work cabanas, each with a sofa and a double bed. The Royal Beach Cabana at the far end adds a swinging bed, a desk and an outdoor shower. Kayaks and canoes are free.

The private beach at The Danai, Sithonia. Courtesy of Splendid Magazine

The table

The Squirrel has five tables on a floor of red marble, wrought-iron chairs with backs shaped like leaves, and a white balustrade with the sun going down into the sea behind it. It is named for the small black squirrels in the pines overhead. Vasilis Mouratidis cooks a set menu with Irish langoustines and Ossetra caviar, and the room has held three stars from Greece’s FNL awards for ten years running. It closes on Mondays, takes no children, and should be booked with the room.

The Andromeda, on a marble terrace under the trees, serves Mediterranean food and a five-course menu that changes daily. The Anithos on the beach grills fish. Breakfast runs under the pines until eleven, built around what The Bakery House baked that morning, and on Sundays it becomes a brunch with live classical music.

Since July the hotel has kept four hives, looked after by Apostolos Melios, a beekeeper since 1998. The honey goes on the breakfast yoghurt and into a spa treatment called Melissa. Danai Riefenstahl is a beekeeper’s daughter.

Most family hotels stop changing long before their fiftieth summer. The Danai spent its own rebuilding the beach and bottling the hillside.

The cellar

The Noble House opened in 2023, 1,500 square metres of sandstone set into the slope like a monastery. Its vaulted wine tunnel runs 130 metres. Inside are some 1,700 labels and more than 18,000 bottles, which the hotel calls the largest private collection in Greece, down to a Heidsieck Monopole from 1907. Apostolos Plahouras has been head sommelier since 2010.

This year the estate made its first wine. Noble Story is a red of Grenache, Mourvèdre and a Syrah variant grown on the hotel’s own slope, and it was opened to start the fiftieth season. The September harvest went into a second vintage. Guests can help with the picking.

Thessaloniki airport is under an hour away. The hotel opens in spring and closes in October, and June and September are the months to aim for, with the sea warm and August gone. In autumn the estate olives, some from trees that stood when the land was bought, are picked by hand and pressed in Nikiti.
