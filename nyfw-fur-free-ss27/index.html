The Season / New York

The fur comes off the schedule

By The Splendid Edit 4 September 2026 Fashion
← Back to The Edit

Backstage next week, a designer's rail will look much as it always has. What changed sits in the rulebook that governs the room.

New York Fashion Week opens on 10 September and runs through the 15th. For the first time, every show on the Official Schedule falls under a fur ban set by the Council of Fashion Designers of America. The CFDA owns and organises the Fashion Calendar, and the ban reaches the calendar, the council's website and its social channels along with the runways themselves.

The council announced the position last December, timing it so designers had months to change materials and show plans before the Spring/Summer 2027 season. That season is now here. It is the first to walk under the rule.

The list

The wording is specific. Farmed or trapped fur from animals killed for their pelts is out, mink and fox and rabbit among them, along with karakul lamb, chinchilla, coyote and raccoon dog. The council named the categories rather than leaving room for interpretation.

One exemption stands. Fur obtained by Indigenous communities through traditional subsistence hunting remains permitted. Everything else on the Official Schedule has to find another material.

For most American designers this is a small adjustment on paper. Steven Kolb, who leads the CFDA, has noted there is already little to no fur shown at New York. The council frames the move as a way to push American designers to think harder about the industry's effect on animals, and to read a consumer base that has been drifting away from fur for years.

The company

New York is late to this rather than early. London ended its promotion of fur in 2023. Copenhagen, Berlin, Stockholm, Amsterdam, Helsinki and Melbourne have done the same. The pressure has also come from the press: Condé Nast, which publishes Vogue, barred animal fur from its editorial and advertising, following earlier moves by ELLE and InStyle.

The council arrived here after years of work with Humane World for Animals and Collective Fashion Justice, both of which had pressed for the change. What is left is Milan and Paris, the two Big Four weeks that still allow fur on their official calendars. The advocates who pushed New York have already named them as next.

What follows

The CFDA has promised more than a prohibition. It plans to give designers educational material and a material library so the search for alternatives has somewhere to start. That is the harder half of the story, and it plays out long after the shows.

Diane von Furstenberg opens the week on the 10th, with Henry Zankov showing his first collection for the house. Thom Browne closes it on the 15th. Between them sit seventy shows and presentations, and not one of them can send fur down the runway. New York has decided what its calendar stands for. The materials that fill the gap are the part still being written.
