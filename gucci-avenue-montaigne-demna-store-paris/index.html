On Avenue Montaigne, Gucci rebuilds in travertine

By The Splendid Edit 2 November 2026 Fashion

Gucci Spring/Summer 2027 campaign. Courtesy of Gucci.

← Back to The Edit

Gucci reopened its Avenue Montaigne flagship on 30 October. It is the first permanent version of the retail concept the house previewed on a Milan runway five weeks earlier, four floors and 1,480 square metres of Italian stone. The clothes will come. The room arrived first.

Gucci showed this room before it built it. On 25 September the house staged The Store Show in Milan, a presentation set inside a sequence of furnished spaces rather than along a plain catwalk. Avenue Montaigne takes that set off the schedule and pours it in stone. The walk a guest made in Milan is now the walk a client makes in Paris.

The concept belongs to Demna, Gucci's artistic director since 2025, and number 56 Avenue Montaigne is the first address where it stands as architecture. The building holds 1,480 square metres across four floors. The brief was to say Italian before a garment is touched, and the materials carry most of that sentence.

Stone

Travertine runs through the rooms in several tones, cut and matched so a floor reads as one material shifting underfoot. It shares the space with Ceppo di Gre, Emperador and Breccia marble, a green stone called Victoria Regia, bronzite, walnut, zebrano and teak. The palette is heavy and worked out in advance. Terrazzo and pigmented concrete fill in the quieter corners.

The references are specific. The design borrows from Italian Renaissance palazzos, from the Milanese rationalism of Piero Portaluppi in the 1930s, and from the interiors of mid-century Milan. None of it reads as pastiche. The stone is the argument, and the argument is that Gucci is an Italian house first. The flagship is a renewal of the boutique Gucci already held on the avenue, reopened now at full scale.

The rooms

There are no departments. The store is laid out as a run of interconnected rooms, each with its own temperature and light, and product is revealed in stages rather than stacked on open shelves. A client moves between moods instead of categories. The plan slows the walk and stages the merchandise the way the September show staged it. Gucci calls the method concealment and revelation, goods kept out of sight until a room decides to show them.

That pacing is the point of the whole exercise. A conventional flagship lets a shopper scan a floor and leave in four minutes. This one asks for a slower pass, room to room, with the goods held back until the space has done its work. Retail theatre is an old idea. Gucci has rebuilt it in marble.

The appartamento

A private space called the Gucci Appartamento sits on the second floor, set aside for a more personal and more exclusive kind of visit. It reads closer to a reception than a sales floor. This is the part that speaks to Kering. Gucci's sales fell six percent on a comparable basis in the third quarter of 2026, and the group has named the brand's recovery as its first priority. Across Kering the quarter held close to flat, Saint Laurent and Bottega Veneta rising while Gucci slipped, which leaves the house the loudest problem in the group to solve.

A store that reserves a floor for its closest clients is a store built to hold the top while the rest comes back. The travertine and the staircase are there for everyone who walks in off the avenue. The appartamento is for the few who return by appointment, and those are the clients a house protects first when the numbers soften.

Subtraction

The opening arrives inside a contraction. Kering has raised its target for store closures across the group to at least 125, part of a plan to trade a wide network for fewer and larger addresses. Avenue Montaigne is the model the survivors are meant to follow.

The concept is built to travel. Gucci has said the elements shown in Paris will be introduced progressively across its international network, so a client who learns this store should recognise the next one on sight. Demna is writing a grammar rather than decorating a single room. The first sentence is now open to the public on Avenue Montaigne.

The read

Demna has staked the reset on the room as much as on the clothes. The store is the first thing a client sees and the first thing a camera frames, and for now those are the same object. Several seasons out, the collections will have to grow into the setting they were handed. For now the setting is the statement, and it is a sure one.

The travertine will outlast a dozen collections. Gucci has bet that the building can hold the house steady until the clothes catch up to it. The doors on Avenue Montaigne are open, and the stone is in place to wait.
