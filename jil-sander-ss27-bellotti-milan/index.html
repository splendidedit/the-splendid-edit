Milan Fashion Week / Spring 2027

At Jil Sander, Bellotti bends the uniform

By The Splendid Edit September 2026 Milan
← Back to The Edit

The show began on time and closed without spectacle. Simone Bellotti sent out clothes that looked severe from the front and came apart, gently, once they moved. His third season at Jil Sander read as a designer settling into a house rather than announcing himself to it.

Bellotti presented Spring/Summer 2027 in Milan on 23 September, three days into a week the city had rebuilt around Prada's new opening slot. He took the Jil Sander bench in early 2025, after Luke and Lucie Meier closed seven years with a fall show and left. His debut arrived that September. This was the follow-through, and it carried the weight a follow-through carries.

The label he inherited holds one of fashion's clearest reputations. Jil Sander means purism, clean lines, tailoring cut back to its own logic. Bellotti spent sixteen years at Gucci under Frida Giannini and then Alessandro Michele, and two more leading Bally, houses that speak at a higher volume. At Jil Sander he has to work in a lower register and still be heard.

His path there ran through the Italian back rooms. Before Gucci he held senior design posts at Dolce & Gabbana, at Bottega Veneta and at Gianfranco Ferré, learning the craft on other people's names. OTB, the Renzo Rosso group that owns Jil Sander alongside Diesel, Marni and Maison Margiela, hired him to run a house rather than assist one. The Meiers had gone after their fall show, announced that February. Bellotti came in with a decade of technique and no public signature of his own. Jil Sander is where he builds one.

The tailoring

He opened with the suit. Jackets closed to the throat. Pockets set high on the chest. Darts placed with the exactness the house is known for, then a shoulder scrunched or a seam pushed off true. The uniform stayed legible. Something small kept interrupting it.

That interruption is the argument. A flawless suit disappears on the body. A suit carrying one deliberate fault holds the eye, and holds the person inside it. Bellotti wants the wearer present in the clothing rather than erased by it, and he builds that wish into the cut instead of the styling.

The color

Then the palette opened. Yellow, blue and red, worn plainly, with no wink attached. Jil Sander has reached for electric color before, though usually as punctuation. Here it filled whole looks. Super-washed silks moved like water down the runway. The leathers held a harder edge, closer to the northern severity the name still carries from its Hamburg beginnings.

Printed skirt sets came in two pieces that almost agreed with each other. Buttons sat mismatched on purpose. Shirts ran large, past the line of fit, into something looser and more lived in. The creased blazer, pressed and then allowed to crumple, returned through the collection like a refrain. None of it read as accident. Each softening had been drawn in advance.

The line

What Bellotti keeps drawing is a line between severity and softness, and he stands on it rather than stepping to either side. The clothes stay strict enough to belong to Jil Sander and loose enough to belong to now. That balance asks more of a designer than either extreme.

A house this bound to a single idea can trap the person running it. Move too far from the purism and the name empties out. Repeat it too faithfully and the work turns into preservation. The Meiers found their own answer across seven years. Bellotti is three collections into finding his, and this one showed him closer to it.

The read

Milan this season was framed as consolidation rather than reinvention. Prada opened the calendar, Maria Grazia Chiuri showed her second Fendi, Demna readied his second Gucci. In that company a third collection from Bellotti is the kind of steady progress the week was built to reward. He is not remaking Jil Sander. He is learning to wear it.

The commercial logic sits underneath the aesthetic one. Minimalism sells when it looks effortless and holds its shape through many washes and many years, and it dies when it looks cheap. OTB needs Jil Sander to read as quiet luxury without tipping into plainness, and that margin is exactly where Bellotti has been working. A washed silk that still hangs right, a leather that keeps its spine, a blazer that crumples on purpose rather than by neglect. The difference is small on the hanger and total on the body.

The test for a house like this never comes at the debut. It comes in the season after the season after, when the novelty has worn off and the clothes have to carry themselves. Spring/Summer 2027 suggested Bellotti understands that. He spent the collection on fit, on color and on one repeated idea, and left the fireworks to labels that need them. Milan took note. So, in time, will the women who end up putting it on.
