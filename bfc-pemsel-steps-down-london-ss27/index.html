The Season / London

David Pemsel's last London

By The Splendid Edit 30 August 2026 Fashion
← Back to The Edit

The British Fashion Council will have a new chair by October. David Pemsel leaves the seat on the thirtieth of September, and the September shows are the last he oversees from the top of the board.

The council confirmed the departure on 27 August. Pemsel joined its executive board in 2016 and took the chair in 2022. Ten years of service, four of them at the head of the table, close with the Spring/Summer 2027 collections. London Fashion Week runs from Thursday 17 September to Monday 21 September. His final day falls nine days after the last show.

A successor is being sought. The council says an announcement will come in September, before Pemsel formally leaves. As of the end of August no name had been made public. Anything said now about who takes the chair, or how the next chair will steer the organisation, is guesswork.

The record

The council frames his tenure in institutional terms. It reports in-year revenue of fourteen million pounds for the 2024/25 financial year, a high for the organisation, and 7.2 million pounds raised through the BFC Foundation. It credits multi-year investment from the Department for Culture, Media and Sport during the same period.

Two decisions sit under his name. The board approved BFC 2030: Access, Creativity, Growth, its first four-year strategy and financial plan. It appointed Laura Weir as chief executive, a role she began in April 2025 after Caroline Rush's long run in the job. By the council's count, Foundation programmes supported seventy-five designers across his time on the board.

Those are the council's own figures, and they belong to a group rather than to one person. Boards, executives, funders and staff produce numbers like these together. The chair sets a direction and holds the room; he does not raise every pound himself.

The season he leaves

The last London under his chairmanship is a heavy one. Seán McGirr shows his first London collection for Alexander McQueen. Christopher Kane presents his first work for Mulberry. Burberry, Erdem, Harris Reed, Richard Quinn, Roksanda and Simone Rocha fill out the calendar, with NEWGEN and Fashion East carrying the newer names underneath.

The council is also stepping outside the capital. Its Fashion Britain programme brings more than a thousand events to Bristol, Dundee, Edinburgh, Liverpool, London, Manchester and Newcastle through September. The aim is stated plainly: wider access, and stronger creative economies in the regions rather than in one city.

The next chair inherits all of it. Not just a runway week, but a funding position, a national programme in its opening phase, and an executive team already building against the 2030 plan. Weir's people are mid-stride. A new chair who moves too fast could pull attention off work that is already running.

The measure of the handover will be dull and slow, the way governance usually is. Whether designer support grows. Whether the regional programme survives past one September. Whether long-term money is secured, and whether London stays commercially useful as well as admired. British fashion enters a new season on the runway and in the boardroom on almost the same week.
