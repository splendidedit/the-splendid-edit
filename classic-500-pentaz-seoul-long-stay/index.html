The Stay · Seoul

A Seoul suite with a washing machine

By Sienna Caldwell 24 November 2026 4 min read
← Back to The Edit

Floors 8 to 20 of a tower beside Konkuk University station are a hotel. Everything above is apartments, and the suites are built for guests who mean to stay a while.

Line 7 stops under the Lotte Department Store. A passage runs beneath the street, through the store and into The Classic 500, so a guest can arrive with suitcases and never feel the weather. Two towers stand here in Gwangjin-gu. The hotel, The Classic 500 Pentaz, takes floors 8 to 20 of Building A, and private residences carry on above it to the 50th.

It opened in May 2012. The name joins the Latin penta, five, to the word zone, and the hotel reads a great deal into the number. The floor plans are plainer. The three largest suite types all measure 125.62 square metres, and the entry Deluxe room is 51.57.

The suite

The Superior Suite has a bedroom, a bathroom with a separate shower, a second toilet, a living room and a full kitchen. The washing machine is in the suite too. An open sleeping area sits off the living room, and with two queens, a super single and a sofa bed, a family fits without anyone on the floor.

The living room is glass from floor to ceiling. From this height Seoul runs out in towers and apartment blocks until the mountains stop it. Morning sun comes straight in, and the long sofa, the armchair and a round white dining table under a glass globe pendant all turn to silhouette.

The lobby at The Classic 500 Pentaz, Gwangjin-gu. Courtesy of Splendid Magazine

The club

The fitness club covers about 2,800 pyeong, a little over 9,000 square metres, threaded through the building. Spa and golf are in the basement, the gym on the fourth floor, an outdoor pool on the fifth. The gym alone is 1,800 square metres of Technogym Artis equipment. Its health assessments run with Konkuk University Hospital up the road: twelve tests, from body composition and baseline fitness to fall risk and vascular age.

The cryotherapy chamber drops to between minus 110 and minus 170 degrees, for sessions under three minutes. The Pilates studio has Gyrotonic and CoreAlign machines. The golf range has 17 bays on GolfZon GDR Plus simulators, with a golf physio centre beside it for players coming back from injury. The spa draws alkaline bedrock water from 830 metres down and runs wet, dry and Roman saunas, a hinoki bath and a salt room.

The Sky Pool is 25 metres long and opens only from late June to the end of August. Hotel guests use the club free, provided they bring their own sportswear and trainers. The gym, spa and golf range are for over-16s. The club closes on the second Wednesday of each month and on New Year's Day, Seollal and Chuseok.

Kondae

The streets around Konkuk University go by Kondae. It is a student district and loud until late, with cheap restaurants and bars packed around the station. The Classic 500 belongs to the Star City complex, so the Lotte Department Store, an E-Mart and a Lotte Cinema are next door. About 200 metres from exit 6, Common Ground stacks 200 blue shipping containers into a shopping centre, labels on the lower floors and food on top, open 11am to 10pm.

Seongsu is one stop away on Line 2. The old shoe-making district has turned its factories and warehouses into cafés, galleries and concept stores, and handmade shoe workshops still work between them. Children's Grand Park is one stop the other way on Line 7.

The palaces are a longer ride, and a first weekend in Seoul belongs closer to them. For a long work trip, or a family that needs a kitchen and a laundry, this is the better address.
