The Season / London

Mulberry comes back to London with Christopher Kane

By The Splendid Edit 18 September 2026 Fashion
← Back to The Edit

Mulberry has not shown on the London schedule since early 2017. On Sunday it returns, with Christopher Kane's name on the collection and a January delivery already fixed. The house is betting on a designer London knows well.

Mulberry has confirmed a show at 15:00 on 20 September. The collection is called Mulberry by Christopher Kane. It reaches stores and the website in January 2027, a gap the house has chosen rather than the usual six-month wait. The label last walked a London runway more than eight years ago, near the end of Johnny Coca's time as creative chief. Coca left in 2020, and the brand held back from the catwalk after that.

The absence has been felt. Mulberry built its modern name on the runway, on bags like the Bayswater and the Alexa that moved from the catwalk into the wider market. Stepping off the schedule left the house selling on heritage alone while newer labels took the London slots and the attention that came with them. A return puts the brand back in front of the editors and buyers who set the season.

The British Fashion Council folded the announcement into a lunch it co-hosted with Mulberry earlier in the summer. Laura Weir, the council's chief executive, described the return as one of her early goals in the job and framed it as a gain for London on creative and commercial terms. Andrew Baldo, Mulberry's chief executive, called the week the right stage for the debut. The room included Kane and his sister Tammy, his collaborator since the start.

Kane

Christopher Kane is not new to any of this. He trained at Central Saint Martins and ran a label on the London schedule for years, one that drew serious attention from the moment it launched. That business sat inside Kering between 2013 and 2018, then closed in 2023. Earlier this year Mulberry named him creative director of a new women's ready-to-wear line, the role he now debuts.

He has designed under someone else's name before, most visibly on Versus alongside Donatella Versace. Mulberry hands him a heritage house with a large bag business and a national following, which is a different task from a signature label. The customer who buys the Bayswater buys it on the name, not the season. Kane has to speak to that customer and to the London front row at the same time.

The schedule

Mulberry rejoins a week that is fuller than it has been in seasons. The British Fashion Council counted 47 catwalk shows and 23 presentations on the SS27 calendar, part of 133 programmed events across five days. Alexander McQueen returns on the same Sunday, for Seán McGirr's first London show as creative director after years of the house presenting in Paris.

Marks & Spencer takes a catwalk slot for the first time, showing menswear and womenswear on a see-now-buy-now basis. Pinterest is staging a phone-free front row with the stylist Claudia Smith. Heritage names, a high-street giant and a platform experiment share the same five days, and the council clearly wants that range read as strength.

The rest of the calendar carries weight of its own. Burberry, Simone Rocha, Erdem, Roksanda, Richard Quinn and Harris Reed anchor the established end, while Aaron Esh, Talia Byre and Chopova Lowena hold ground for the younger names the city keeps producing. Mulberry lands in that company as a known quantity with something to prove, which is a useful position on a crowded schedule.

The stakes

Mulberry has spent recent years working through soft demand and a reset of its wholesale and retail footprint. A runway return tells buyers and press that the creative direction has an owner and a face again. The January delivery matters here. The clothes arrive close to when they are seen, which suits a house that sells leather goods in volume and needs the show to convert.

Kane's job is to make those leather goods feel considered again without losing the shopper who already trusts the brand. London has watched him handle the first part, across a decade of his own collections. The second part, scale with a mass following attached, is the new problem. Sunday is where he starts to answer it.

There is a pattern behind the week. London has spent seasons watching its biggest houses decamp to Paris and Milan for the reach those cities promise. McGirr's McQueen returning, and Mulberry rejoining the schedule the same afternoon, reads as a small reversal of that drift. The council is treating both shows as proof that the city can still hold names at that level.

The SS27 schedule reads like a city arguing for itself once more, after seasons of talk about London losing its biggest names. Mulberry's return is the clearest line in that argument. At three o'clock on Sunday it stops being a plan.
